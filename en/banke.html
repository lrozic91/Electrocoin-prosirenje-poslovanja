Crypto module for banks

A bank's customer buys, sells and holds crypto directly in their bank's app, while Electrocoin provides the service in the background. Electrocoin already offers such a module to banks1, and no Croatian bank yet offers crypto trading2.

We target two banks. HPB, because Croatian Post is a co-owner and HPB sells its retail services through post offices34, and Electrocoin has worked with Croatian Post since 2019.5 Zagrebačka banka, because it is the largest bank in Croatia6.

1. Does Electrocoin's MiCA licence cover this business

On 9 Apr 2026 Hanfa authorised Electrocoin for three of the ten MiCA services78
MiCA service9What it means in the moduleDoes Electrocoin have it?
(a) Custody of crypto-assetsThe bank's customer holds crypto in the app.Yes7
(c) Exchange of crypto for fundsBuying and selling crypto for euros. Electrocoin is the counterparty in each purchase and sale.Yes7
(d) Exchange of crypto for other cryptoSwaps, e.g. bitcoin for ether.Yes7
(j) Transfer services for crypto-assetsSending crypto to, or receiving it from, an external wallet.No. Needed only if the module allows sending and receiving.
(e) Execution of orders on behalf of clientsElectrocoin, as an intermediary, routes orders to other exchanges.No. Needed only if Electrocoin were not the counterparty to the trade10.
  • Conclusion: the licence already covers the basic module: buying, selling, swapping and holding crypto, provided Electrocoin is the counterparty in every trade.
  • For the full module with sending and receiving crypto, Electrocoin needs to ask Hanfa to extend its licence to service (j), transfer of crypto-assets.
  • Valid outside Croatia too: the licence is passported to 30 EEA countries11, and France, for example, has confirmed this12.
  • The bank's role: MiCA does not allow a bank to act merely as Electrocoin's agent receiving orders13. So the bank either only provides access within its app (model A), or provides the service itself after notifying Hanfa (model B)1415.

Two models of partnership with a bank

Both are already in use in Europe
A. Electrocoin provides the service, the bank provides accessB. The bank provides the service, Electrocoin is the technology partner
How it worksThe customer becomes Electrocoin's client within the bank's app. Electrocoin buys, sells and holds the crypto, and pays the bank a fee for access.The bank is the service provider to the customer. Electrocoin supplies the technology, pricing and crypto custody for the bank16.
What the bank needsNo notification to Hanfa. A contract and due diligence on Electrocoin as a partner are required17.Notification to Hanfa at least 40 working days before launch, with policies, systems and procedures1415. Plus due diligence on the partner17.
What Electrocoin needsThe existing licence is sufficient for buying, selling and custody7.The existing licence is sufficient for pricing and custody7. Responsibility towards the customer stays with the bank16.
Real-world exampleRaiffeisen banks in Austria with Bitpanda: the bank provides access and Bitpanda pays it a fee18.German cooperative banks (DZ Bank) and Sparkassen: the bank holds the authorisation, and the partner holds the crypto and provides pricing1920.

Proposal: model A for a pilot project with HPB, as it is the quickest and the bank does not need to notify Hanfa. Banks do not want to hold crypto on their own balance sheet, as they would need far more capital against it21. In both models the crypto stays with Electrocoin, so the bank avoids this problem.

3. Build, roll-out and maintenance costs

For Electrocoin, in two staffing variants. All figures in this section are estimates
  • Starting point: Electrocoin already has buying, selling and custody of crypto, so it is not building from scratch. For a bank that builds everything itself, this costs $0.5–2M and 12–24 months45.
  • Variant 1, existing staff do the work: no additional staff costs.
  • Variant 2, 2 new hires dedicated to this project: total cost ~€75–122k gross per year4748.
  • Extending the licence to crypto transfers: the amount is not public.

Turnover and revenue estimate

All figures in this section are estimates, not data
  • Share of customers using the module: at PostFinance, a postal bank, ~1.4% of customers use crypto after two years2425. The default is 2%, because 16% of Croatians own or have owned crypto, almost three times the EU average44.
  • Turnover per user: there is no public data for banks. In 2025 Bitpanda had €371M in revenue across 7.4M users49, i.e. ~€50 per user. At a fee of ~1.5%, that is ~€3,300 in turnover per year. The default is €3,000.
  • Customer fee: banks charge 0.95–2.5%233536. The default is 1.5%.
  • Electrocoin's share of the fee: the split between bank and partner is not public18. The default is 50%.
  • Model A or model B: the calculation applies to both models. Turnover is the same; only the share of the fee that Electrocoin keeps differs. In model A Electrocoin charges the customer the fee and pays part of it to the bank for access, so its share would be higher, probably above 50% (estimate). In model B the bank charges the fee and pays Electrocoin for technology and custody, so its share would be lower, probably below 50% (estimate). The default of 50% is the midpoint.

What to watch out for

  • Before signing, the bank will vet Electrocoin thoroughly as a partner: security, business continuity and exit strategy17. The documentation for this should be prepared in advance.
  • The line between the bank merely providing access and the bank receiving orders should be confirmed in writing with Hanfa13.
  • Zagrebačka banka offered the bitcoin certificate only in branches, with a suitability interview37. This means it is cautious, but interested in crypto.

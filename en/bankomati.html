Taking over crypto ATMs

Bitomat lists 17 ATMs in 13 Croatian cities1, but they are currently out of service because the operator has no MiCA licence24. Since 1 July 2026, only licensed firms may provide crypto services in Croatia3. Electrocoin holds a licence to exchange crypto for cash4, so it can bring these ATMs back into service, either as owner or purely as operator.

Who is behind Bitomat

Who Electrocoin would be negotiating with
  • Croatian company: Virtual Planet d.o.o. of Zagreb, founded in 2020, with a single founder and director from Poland6. It is named as the operator of the Shitcoins.club ATMs in Croatia7, and in 2022 Hanfa issued it a decision registering it as a virtual currency exchange8.
  • Owner of the Bitomat.com platform: Virtual Planet EOOD of Plovdiv, Bulgaria5. Until 2024 it was run by the same person as the Croatian company; today its owner is Wojciech Gramowski of Poland9.
  • Licence: neither of these companies is in Hanfa's register of licensed providers4. The same network has also suspended operations in Italy for lack of MiCA authorisation10.
  • Revenue in Croatia: in 2025, Virtual Planet d.o.o. had only €8,848 in revenue and no employees6. ATM turnover is probably not booked in the Croatian company, so FINA records cannot tell us how much business the ATMs did.

Negotiating position: the ATMs are idle and earning nothing, and the owner cannot restart them without a licence. This works in Electrocoin's favour whether it buys them or agrees a lease.

Estimate: turnover and revenue of 17 ATMs

All figures in this section are estimates, not data
  • Turnover: turnover figures for Bitomat ATMs in Croatia are not public. As a reference point, older European estimates are around $20,000–30,000 per ATM per month17. The default is €20,000 per ATM per month, and you can change it with the slider.
  • Fee: Bitomat charges an average of 2–4% on buying and selling1112. The default is 4%, the average as of November 2025.11
  • Costs (option A): ~$300–650 per ATM per month (estimate, excluding customer checks)14. The default is €450.
  • Fee share under a lease (option B): how much of the fee Electrocoin keeps depends on negotiation. The default is 50%.

Points to watch

  • With low fees of 2–4% and costs of ~$300–650 per ATM per month, buying only pays off if the ATMs have enough turnover. A lease is safer, because Electrocoin does not bear the cost of the machines and locations.
  • Before any deal, the ATMs' actual turnover from previous years must be seen. The Croatian company does not report it6, so it has to be requested from the owner.
  • The network also sells its own ATMs11, so it needs checking which software they run and whether it can be connected to Electrocoin's system.
  • The ATMs handle cash, so under its licence Electrocoin also takes on responsibility for anti-money-laundering controls on every machine.
